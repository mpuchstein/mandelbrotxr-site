The reviewers' one-line reasons, verbatim

A GPT6 Pro · v0

  • Done-when fulfilment 8 — Immersive VR, full-size AR and tabletop AR rooms are available through native WebXR. iPhones get a 3D touch, motion and stereo room, which is the best possible without WebXR on iOS.
  • Device/XR compatibility 8 — Correct feature detection, local-floor/hand-tracking/dom-overlay requested as optional, four input sources (controllers, hands, transient-pointer, screen taps) and a secure-context check. There is no hit-test and no hand meshes.
  • Visual wow effect 6 — A well-composed gallery with signage, a live minimap wall and a glass case. The fractal itself is a coarse vertex-coloured heightfield (at most 289×217 vertices) with smoothed cliff normals and no env map.
  • Usability & controls 8 — Excellent 2D UI, guide modal, minimap and presets, plus an in-world 9-button panel, teleport rings and snap turn. The VR panel is low (centre at 0.65 m), there is no hover feedback, and the AR tabletop panel sits in front of the exhibit.
  • Code quality & robustness 7 — Robust: worker with fallback, generation IDs, transferables, context-loss handling and user activation preserved for requestSession. However, the app code ships minified (single-letter identifiers), and palette changes trigger full recomputes.
  • Prompt adherence 8 — Truly standalone (three r186 embedded, zero network dependencies). three.xr.js is replaced by WebXRManager, with the reason explained in the in-page guide (three.xr.js is archived and uses obsolete APIs).

B GPT6 Pro · v1

  • Done-when fulfilment 8 — Quest/Pico/PCVR enter an immersive-vr room with a 3D escape-time relief. Android gets immersive-ar (a 6DoF window into the room). iPhone gets a non-immersive touch/gyro 3D view, the best available without WebXR on iOS.
  • Device/XR compatibility 8 — VR+AR, local-floor→local fallback, hand-tracking, transient-pointer, gaze-dwell, snap turn, teleport, secure-context and iOS motion-permission handling. AR is opaque and dom-overlay is dead code.
  • Visual wow effect 7 — Custom contour/rim/spec terrain shader, noise nebula sky, star and spark particles, glowing rings, canvas signage. No env map (dull metals), no bloom, a 2.5D heightfield, and low resolution in XR.
  • Usability & controls 7 — Polished 2D UI, presets, help/XR dialogs, a 9-button in-world panel with grip-summon. In XR you can only zoom at the centre, and the set's plateau sits above eye height.
  • Code quality & robustness 7 — Worker + main-thread fallback, cancellation tokens, adaptive quality, error mapping, disposal. But the app code ships minified, with about 120 un-instanced room draw calls and some dead paths.
  • Prompt adherence 9 — One self-contained 656 KB file with three.js r186 embedded, zero network requests, WebXR as asked.

The reviewers' one-line reasons, verbatim

A GPT6 Pro · v0

  • Loads & renders 9 — Load event at ~136 ms, 3D room and relief visible at first screenshot; zero console messages across every session.
  • Done-when (desktop-checkable) 8 — A real gallery room around a 3D Mandelbrot relief on a plinth; the XR path is present but correctly disabled here. It deliberately does not use three.xr.js, and says why.
  • Visual wow 8 — Polished "observatory" look: plinth, glass case, wall plaques, halo ring, three palettes. Zoomed presets look weaker (thin floating slab).
  • Usability & controls 8 — Orbit, scroll, walk mode with WASD and an on-screen pad, minimap click-to-center, presets, palettes, detail and relief controls, plus a thorough guide. On narrow screens the Explore panel hides the view, and the header icons are 34 px.
  • Robustness 8 — No errors. Unavailable fullscreen and motion sensors are handled with clear toasts, and stereo exits cleanly. Frame rate could not be measured in this environment (see notes).
  • XR readiness & messaging 8 — isSessionSupported gating; "VR unavailable here" is a disabled button with a plain explanation. AR (room and tabletop) is hidden when unsupported. Split-screen stereo and a gyro fallback are offered. Untestable without a device.
Browser review (blind id candidate-06), criteria table, verbatim — REVIEW.md lines 5–10 GitHub

B GPT6 Pro · v1

  • Loads & renders 9 — A finished 3D frame appears in under 1 s, with zero console messages (errors or warnings) across the whole session. It is one self-contained inline script on WebGL 2 with no CDN dependencies.
  • Done-when (desktop-checkable) 8 — It is a real room: a circular observatory with pillars, rings, wall typography, a plinth and a 3D escape-time heightfield of the set. The XR path exists (a hidden "Enter AR" button and a device-capability dialog), but entering XR can't be confirmed without a device.
  • Visual wow effect 8 — The overview is striking: neon contour lines, glowing rings, layered terraces and three good palettes. It is weaker at zoomed destinations, where a square slab overhangs the round plinth and the set interior is a dark flat plateau.
  • Usability & controls 8 — Destinations, palette, orbit, zoom, home, sliders, walk mode, keyboard shortcuts and an excellent help sheet all work. The zoom buttons can drop you into a featureless flat area, and the HUD copy overlaps text in the 3D scene.
  • Robustness 7 — No errors, the canvas tracks resizes, and Home/R recovers from any state. Fullscreen didn't engage here and gave no feedback, a first H press was swallowed by button focus, and frame rate couldn't be measured (the harness throttled requestAnimationFrame).
  • XR readiness & messaging 9 — The "XR info" dialog lists 3D room / VR / AR / Connection status and explains HTTPS, Quest/PICO/Steam Frame/iPhone support and in-headset controls (teleport, snap turn, gaze-dwell). The Enter buttons are hidden when unsupported, and the copy is clear and honest.
