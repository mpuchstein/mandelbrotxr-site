How to read this

  • Rank 1 = best. Each judge column is one judge’s blind ranking of all candidates it saw. A dash (–) means that judge did not rank that candidate.
  • Mean rank derived is the arithmetic mean of a candidate’s ranks over the judges that ranked it (over the judges currently shown, when you filter by material). Equal means are ties and are shown as ties: “=1” means shared first place. The standing (#) is only given to candidates ranked by every judge; a candidate that some judges did not rank shows how many did (e.g. “4/5 judges”) and is listed after the others.
  • Spread derived gives the best and worst rank a candidate received (min–max) and σ, the population standard deviation of its ranks across judges: σ = √(Σ(r − mean)² / n). A large σ marks a controversial candidate. In the glyph, the faint end ticks mark rank 1 (left) and rank 8 (right), as labelled in the column header; each solid dot is one judge (equal ranks stack vertically) and the light full-height tick behind the dots is the mean.
  • Phase-1 scores are the 1–10 overall scores from separate blind reviews (one reviewer per review type and candidate), not from the judges. Click a score to read the review.
  • Judge material: “browser”, “static” — static judges read code and/or transcripts only; browser judges used the product hands-on in desktop Chrome.
  • v0 and v1 are different prompts; each candidate was judged against its own prompt, in one combined ranking.

Read with care. Methodology and caveats.

  • Two prompts. v0 and v1 are different prompts (v0 asks for three.js and three.xr.js; v1 asks for WebXR and the "highest possible visual wow effect"). Each candidate was judged against its own prompt, in one combined ranking.
  • No headset. No real VR or AR session was entered. Everything was tested in desktop Chrome, without an XR device or emulator; phone widths were simulated.
  • Same model family. All reviewers and judges were Opus 5.5 agents, and two of the eight candidates are Opus 5.5 builds.

How much do the judges agree? derived

Kendall’s τb between every pair of judges, computed over the candidates both judges ranked (n per pair):

τb = (C − D) / √((C + D + Tx)(C + D + Ty))

C and D are the numbers of candidate pairs the two judges order the same way (concordant) or the opposite way (discordant); Tx and Ty count pairs tied in only one of the two rankings (rankings here have no ties, so τb equals plain Kendall τ). The last column averages a judge’s τ with all the others.

Reading guide: 1 = identical order, 0 = unrelated, −1 = exactly reversed; the stronger the cell colour, the stronger the agreement. Here the closest pair is HTML and Browser + HTML (τ = 1.00); the least similar is Transcript and Browser (τ = 0.50).
